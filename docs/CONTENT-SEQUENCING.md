# Content Sequencing — Stage A–D Curriculum

Turns the Stage A–D learning flow into a concrete, ordered plan of *what to
introduce, in what order*. Built on the app's real data:

- Words per band: HSK1 **153** · HSK2 **170** · HSK3 **259** · HSK4 **581**
  (plus Boya L11–L14 and GCP7 L6 as supplements).
- 14 sound-clue families and 47 meaning radicals already in the data.

Two rules from the learning-science design govern everything:
1. **Encode components before whole words.** A word made of already-known parts
   is one chunk to learn, not many.
2. **The word is the unit of testing and scheduling; the character is the unit
   of encoding.**

Intake is capped (≤ ~8–12 new words/day) and everything is interleaved and
FSRS-scheduled. The sequence below is the *introduction order*; review of earlier
items keeps running mixed in.

---

## STAGE A — Foundation (HSK 1–2, ~320 words)

### A0. The building blocks (encode these first, ~2 weeks)

Before words, front-load the components that most later characters are built
from. These pay for themselves many times over.

**~14 core meaning radicals** (highest frequency in your data), each with what it
signals:

氵 water · 讠 speech · 口 mouth · 亻 person · 扌 hand · 木 tree/wood ·
女 woman · 忄/心 heart · 日 sun/day · 月 body/moon · 辶 walking/road ·
艹 grass/plant · 宀 roof · 土 earth

**~16 building-block characters** (frequent, and themselves components), taught
with their honest decomposition:

一 二 三 十 (numbers) · 人 大 小 · 口 目 (mouth, eye) · 女 子 好(女+子) ·
木 日 月 · 山 中 上 下

Goal of A0: when a new character appears, the learner already recognizes its
parts, so the memory story (§ decomposition) is *assembling known pieces*, not
memorizing a new shape.

### A1. Sound-clue families (teach as chunks, woven through A)

Introduce each family as a set: learn the sound component once, then its members
come cheaply. Order by yield (member count and word usefulness), using your data:

1. 巴 (bā) → 吧 爸 把
2. 也 (yě) → 他 她 地
3. 元 (yuán) → 远 完 玩 园
4. 马 (mǎ) → 妈 吗
5. 青 (qīng) → 请 晴 睛
6. 主 (zhǔ) → 住 往
7. 己 (jǐ) → 记 起
8. 可 (kě) → 哥 歌
9. 生 (shēng) → 姓 星
10. singles as they come up: 果→课, 中→钟, 票→漂, 那→哪, 快→筷

Teach the family the day its first member is due; the others become "known sound
+ a radical."

### A2. Tones (an ordered skill, from day 1)

Tones are a fixed 1–2–3–4 set — the one place the method of loci helps. Drill the
four contours as a sequence, then test every new word's tone pattern explicitly.
Do not let tones be "picked up" incidentally; they must be retrieved.

### A3. HSK 1 words, then HSK 2 (the spine of Stage A)

- Introduce HSK 1 in frequency order (most-recombining characters first). At
  ~10 new/day, the 153 HSK-1 words seed over ~2–3 weeks *of introductions*, with
  reviews interleaved — total elapsed time depends on retention, not a calendar.
- Task mix in Stage A: **recognition + listening** dominate; a word graduates to
  **recall (production)** after 2–3 clean reps.
- Only after HSK 1 words are mostly *recognized* do HSK 2 words enter — and HSK 1
  words simultaneously shift into production. Never block; interleave the two.

**Exit gate A:** ≥ 90% retention on HSK 1 in *production* (meaning → 汉字), and
the 14 sound families recognized on sight.

---

## STAGE B — Build (HSK 2–3, to ~900 words)

### B1. Shift the retrieval mode

Production becomes the **default** for any maturing word. Recognition is only for
the newest items. This is the generation-effect payoff.

### B2. Introduce grammar patterns as reusable frames

Words must become *usable*. Add patterns from your grammar set as words allow,
roughly in this order of utility:

把 … (disposal) · 了 (change/completion) · 越 … 越 … · 要是 … 就 … ·
比 (comparison) · 一边 … 一边 … · 从 … 到 …

Each pattern is taught with example sentences built only from words the learner
already knows (comprehensible frames).

### B3. Add sentence cloze

Once a word is recognized, graduate it to **fill-the-blank in a real sentence**
(encoding specificity). Gate cloze to recognized words so it stays retrieval, not
decoding.

### B4. Intake gating

Keep new-word intake throttled by due load: heavy review day → fewer new words.
Retention beats growth. Interleave HSK 1 (production) + HSK 2 (production) +
HSK 3 (recognition).

**Exit gate B:** can *produce*, not just recognize, HSK 1–2; comfortable with the
core grammar frames in cloze.

---

## STAGE C — Consolidate (HSK 4, to ~1,200 words)

HSK 4 is the largest band (581 words), so this is the longest stage and the most
context-heavy.

- **Production + context dominate:** sentence cloze and listening for mature
  words; recall for maturing; recognition only for the newest.
- **Maximum interleaving** across all four HSK bands in every session.
- **Leech focus:** words that resist get extra elaboration (re-show the character
  story, break the word into its known parts) and tighter spacing — not just more
  raw repetitions.
- Character-story leverage: by now most new HSK-4 characters are *combinations of
  known components*, so decomposition encoding is fast.

**Exit gate C:** mock HSK-4 vocabulary pass — recognize and produce the band at
≥ 90% held retention.

---

## STAGE D — Maintain

- Goal flips from adding to **holding**. Pure spaced production, mixed bands, low
  daily load.
- New material only from supplements (Boya, GCP, or uploaded lessons), gated hard
  so maintenance load stays light.
- Success = ≥ 90% retention across HSK 1–4 in < ~15 min/day.

---

## First four weeks (a concrete Stage-A example)

Illustrative, assuming ~10 new words/day and daily review:

- **Week 1:** A0 radicals + building-block characters (encode the parts). Begin
  HSK 1 words (recognition + listening). Tone drill daily. Sound families 1–3
  (巴, 也, 元) as their members appear.
- **Week 2:** Continue HSK 1 introductions. First HSK-1 words graduate to recall.
  Sound families 4–6 (马, 青, 主). Keep interleaving old + new.
- **Week 3:** Finish HSK 1 introductions; HSK 1 now mostly in production. Begin
  HSK 2 (recognition). Sound families 7–9 (己, 可, 生). First cloze sentences on
  solid HSK-1 words.
- **Week 4:** HSK 2 introductions continue, interleaved with HSK 1 production.
  First grammar frames (把, 了). Assess exit gate A.

Elapsed time is retention-driven — if reviews pile up, slow new intake. The
schedule protects memory, not a calendar.

## Intake and mix, by stage (summary)

| Stage | New/day | Dominant task | Also | Grammar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | ~8–12 | recognition + listening | tone; recall after 2–3 reps | — |
| B | ~10, gated | recall (production) | cloze; listening | core frames |
| C | ~8, gated | production + context | cloze; listening; tone | broader frames |
| D | ~0–5 | spaced production | mixed, interleaved | maintain |

## How this maps to the app you built

The daily session already implements most of this: FSRS scheduling, new-word
gating by due load and a per-day cap, interleaving, task selection that shifts
from recognition → recall → tone/sentence as a word matures, and immediate
feedback.

The three *content* additions this plan called for are now **all built and
tested** (shipped in js/session.js + js/logic.js):

1. **[✅ Implemented]** Front-load an explicit **A0 "building blocks" set** before
   HSK 1 words. The 15 component/pictograph characters that exist as cards
   (一 二 三 十 人 大 小 口 好 日 月 上 下 火 水) are an ordered priority list; a
   pure, unit-tested helper (`Logic.orderNewByPriority`) pulls them to the front
   of new-word selection, so a fresh learner meets the building blocks first.
2. **[✅ Implemented]** Surface **sound families at first encounter**. When a new
   word's character belongs to a phonetic family, its first recognition card
   shows a "Sound clue" line naming the shared component and its siblings
   (e.g. 请 → "青 (qīng) → 请 晴 睛") — teaching the chunk, once.
3. **[✅ Implemented]** **Tones as an explicit ordered drill** from day one. A
   four-tone primer (妈 麻 马 骂, ordered 1→2→3→4, each tappable to hear) leads a
   new learner's first three sessions, then retires automatically.

Everything else — the scheduling, interleaving, production shift, cloze, leech
handling — was already in place.
