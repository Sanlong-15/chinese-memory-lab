Sound-clue families (形声字)

Each group below shares one component that gives the SOUND. A different piece attached to it gives the MEANING category. Learn the group once, and you basically get 2-4 characters for free.

Meaning-clue families (radicals)

These don't share a sound — they share a MEANING radical. Once you recognize a radical, you can guess roughly what a new character is about, even before you know how to say it. Each card below gives the radical's own sound, its Chinese name, where it sits inside a character, and what it tells you. Tap any character to hear it and jump to its word card.

Character Structures (汉字结构)

Every Chinese character fits one of a few shapes. Before you write a character, see its shape first: does it split left–right, stack top–bottom, or wrap around? Knowing the shape tells you the stroke order and helps you remember the parts. Left–right and top–bottom are by far the most common. Tap any example character to hear it and open its word.

Grammar Patterns

Words are bricks; grammar is the glue. Each card shows one common pattern — the shape of the sentence, a simple rule, and real examples using words you already know. Tap an example to hear it. Learn these and you can start building your own sentences, not just recognizing words.

Sentence Practice

A word is hidden in a real sentence. Read the English meaning, then choose the missing word. This trains words the way you actually use them — inside sentences, not alone. After you answer, the blank fills in and you hear the whole sentence.

Listening Practice

The word is spoken, not shown. Listen, then choose the meaning. This trains your ears — your weakest skill for real conversation. Tap "Play again" if you need to hear it once more.

Tone Practice

The word is spoken. Listen to the tones and pick the pattern. Tones: 1 flat & high, 2 rising, 3 dips down then up, 4 falling sharp. Wrong tones are the top reason people misunderstand you.
